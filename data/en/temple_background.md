# CATEGORY: history
## Kalasa's legendary history and dynasty

Our present-day Kalasa was an intact state centuries ago, the capital of that state. The kings here were first Chalukyas, Hoysalas and then Vijayanagara kings.

At the beginning of the 14th century, the kingdom of Kalasa was ruled by **Loknath Devarasa**, a Mandalika of the Hoysalas. The descendants of Santhara used to add 'Bhairava' to their name. Hence the subjects used to call him 'Bhairava Aras'. It gradually became **Bhairarasa**.

Kalasa was a great commercial center under Bhairarasar's rule. The traders here had their business in Karkala, Moodabitha etc. At that time, Tundarasa named 'Heggade', who was in charge of the administration of Karkala and 6 nearby countries, was very troublesome. The Vainikas of the Karkala country could not bear Heggade's subterfuge and complained to the king of Kalasa who had come to Moodabi. Kalasa Bhairarasa brought his army and defeated the king of Karkala and annexed those lands to his kingdom. The Hoysala emperor Ballala III appointed Lokanatha Devarasa as the Kalasa-Mahamandaleswara of the Karkala kingdom.

Around 1650, Keladi II Venkatappa Nayaka invaded and defeated Bhairarasa III and annexed the Kalasa-Karkala kingdom to the Keladi kingdom. At that time the kingdom of Bhairaras, which had been in existence for about 500 years, became undisturbed. Further in 1763 Hyderali won Keladi state and added it to Mysore state, in 1799 British captured Srirangapatna.

The Bhairaras of Kalasa, though Jains, were extremely tolerant. In 1516, Immadi Bhairarasa, who was the Mandalika of Vijayanagara, renovated the **Sri KalasaShwara temple** which was in a state of disrepair.

# CATEGORY: migration
## Gowda Saraswatas journey

The Gauda Saraswata Brahmins who settled in Goa from the Gauda country of North India (now Bengal) maintained their distinctiveness there. For the first time in AD In 1098 and later in 1310 and 1469, they were attacked by the Bijapur Sultans.

Goa was captured by the Portuguese in 1510. To escape the persecution and forced conversions of the Portuguese, the devotees migrated to the coastal and inland areas on the Ghats with the idols of their family gods.

These Gauda Saraswata came into contact with the Brahmins when Kalasa merchants went to the coastal towns for trade. Acquainted by them, Gowda Saraswatas found accommodation in Kalasa's loft for employment. They built a temple for themselves and for their beloved **Sri Venkataramana Swami**, enshrined Sri Swami and came to worship.

# CATEGORY: kalasa
## Dakshina Kashi - Our Kalasa

Our town is surrounded by the river from three sides and this place is named Kalasa because it is shaped like Kalasa.

When Agastya Maharishi was unable to participate in Vishwanath's welfare festival of Kashi, Parameshwara blessed him to see his regular welfare festival in Kalasa field. So Kalasa is called **'Dakshina Kashi'**.

Like Kashi on the banks of the Ganges in northern India, Kalasa on the banks of the Bhadra in southern India is a holy shrine. Surrounding Kalasa are **Rudratirtha, Kotitirtha, Ambatirtha, Bhandaru tirtha and Vasishtha tirthas**, sanctifying this area.
